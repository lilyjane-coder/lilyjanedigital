The fire isn't what kills them

Eight slides for tomorrow. Tragedy pillar, live-news timing, lands on the safety lane. Every figure below is sourced and dated — check the notes before you publish.

Why this one, and why tomorrow

Your instinct on the wildfire angle was the right call — it's a stronger post than the moose re-cut, and it's the one that decays if you wait.

  • It's live news. 109 fires are burning in B.C. right now. That's the same ingredient that made the Grinder post work — you're answering a question people are already asking.
  • It's your pillar, done right. Not "bears die" — a named bear with a story, at stakes. Grizzly 222 walking her cubs out of the Jasper fire is the emotional spine.
  • It converts. The grief lands on "secure your attractants," which is the account's actual job. Tragedy in, safety out.
  • The hook inverts an assumption. Same engine as your 6K "oversized raccoons" post — the reader is wrong about something, and the swipe tells them how.

The slides

Copy is final — lift it as written. Mockups show placement and emphasis; drop your photo behind each.

Slide 1 · hook

Everyone pictures bears dying in the flames.

That's not how a wildfire kills a bear.

Image

Wall of fire, or a bear silhouetted against orange smoke. Most dramatic frame you have — this slide's only job is the stop.

Why

States a surprising claim but withholds the mechanism. That's the gap the swipe closes — the thing your moose slide 1 gave away.

Slide 2 · credibility

3%

Researchers pulled together 31 studies that tracked collared animals straight through a wildfire. On average, the fire itself killed three percent of them.

Image

Quiet burned forest, black trunks. Let the number carry it.

Source

Nimmo et al., Global Change Biology 2022 — 31 species, 23 families, 1984–2020. Mean predicted mortality 3% (CI 1–9%). Severe fires 7%, mild 2%.

Slide 3 · the named bear

During the Jasper fire, a grizzly called 222 took her two cubs down to the Athabasca River and waited it out.

Her GPS collar tracked the whole thing. All three walked away.

Image

Sow with cubs, or a river running through burned country. Warmest image in the deck — this is the hope beat.

Why

Your pillar is named bears with stories. This is the slide people screenshot, and it sets up the drop on slide 4.

Slide 4 · the turn

Then the smoke clears, and she walks her cubs back into a home range with nothing left in it.

No berries. No roots. No cover.

And winter is nine weeks out.

Image

Wide burn scar, ash, no green. Coldest, emptiest frame you have.

Why

The pivot. Slide 3 gave relief, this takes it back — that whiplash is what drives comments.

Slide 5 · mechanism

A bear that can't fatten up doesn't survive hibernation.

So it stops looking in the burn and starts looking downhill — orchards, campgrounds, green bins.

Image

Bear on a road, near a fence line, or at the edge of a subdivision.

Why

Where the post stops being about fire and starts being about us. Don't rush this line.

Slide 6 · the cost

In 2023 — B.C.'s worst fire and drought year on record — conservation officers killed 603 black bears.

The most in a decade.

Image

Sombre. Empty green bin, garbage bags at a curb, or a road at dusk. Not a dead bear.

Source

CBC / BCCOS: 460 killed in the first 9 months of 2023 across 21,000 conflict reports; 603 for the full year.

Careful

Say 2023 out loud. 2025 was a 15-year low — if you imply kills are peaking right now, someone will correct you in the comments and you'll lose the room.

Slide 7 · the orphans

That same autumn, one shelter in Smithers took in more than 100 orphaned cubs.

The most in 33 years of operating.

Image

Cub. Any cub. This is the most shareable slide in the deck.

Source

Northern Lights Wildlife Society, Smithers — 100+ cubs by end of 2023 after the drought and fire season, peaking above 120.

Slide 8 · the ask

109 fires are burning in B.C. today

The bears will survive the fire.

What they might not survive is what we leave out for them when they come looking.

Lock the bins. Pick the fruit. Feeders down until November.

Image

Your own trail footage or a clean landscape. Bring it home to where the viewer lives.

Why

"A fed bear is a dead bear" stays unsaid — you've just spent seven slides proving it, and letting the audience land it themselves is what gets it typed into the comments.

Check the fire count the morning you post. The 109 figure is from Aug 1. If it's moved, update slide 8 — a stale number on the one slide that says "today" is the kind of thing that gets caught.

Caption

Grizzly 222 walked her cubs to the river and survived the Jasper fire. It's what came after that nearly killed them. 🐻‍❄️🔥

With 109 fires burning in B.C. right now, this is the year to be relentless about attractants. Bins locked, fruit picked, feeders down.
